# Synthesize

Help the reader understand what the material changes about a problem, why that judgment follows, and what can be reused. Do the selection and reasoning in the text so the reader does not reconstruct it from scattered notes or links.

## Choose the question worth answering

Infer the reader and purpose from the request; ask only when the missing distinction would materially change the content. A single fragment of thinking is as valid an input as a set of Runs. Identify the problem, the earlier understanding or alternatives, and the uncertainty that matters now. Select content by its effect on understanding or a decision, not by effort spent or chronology. Keep a negative result, contradiction, or limitation whenever omitting it would change the meaning.

Adapt the emphasis to the use:

- Organizing ideas: connect the objective, assumptions, tensions, and options; separate a useful direction from an untested explanation.
- Experiment summary: what comparison was made, whether it was valid, and how far it supports or challenges the claim.
- Research notes: changes in understanding, plausible mechanisms, counterexamples, and methods that may help related problems.
- Weekly report: meaningful progress, findings, and decisions tied to the goal and the next work.

The same evidence keeps the same factual meaning across audiences.

## Relate evidence to judgment

Read sources before interpreting them. Identify which observations support each other, answer different parts of the question, conflict, or cannot be compared, and check the conditions that decide this: evaluation criteria, data, baseline, operating assumptions. Do not average incompatible results or keep only the favorable ones.

State the change in understanding when the material supports one: what was learned, which interpretation became more or less credible, which option was excluded, what remains open. If the notes show only completed work or an unchanged uncertainty, say so; do not manufacture a discovery. Treat a source author's conclusion as a claim to check against its evidence. When results disagree without an established reason, keep the disagreement and name what would distinguish the explanations. Insufficient evidence for a benefit does not show a method can never work.

## Extract lessons that travel

Keep a lesson only when it changes what someone would do in a similar situation. Separate the case-specific conditions from the reasoning that produced the judgment, test the lesson against a counterexample or condition change from the material, and state where it applies, the action it recommends, and what would require adjusting it. Match its strength to the evidence: one observation supports a local caution or a hypothesis, not a rule. Replace vague advice such as "improve experimental discipline" with the concrete decision it should change.

## Deliver

Lead with the main understanding, then the facts and reasons needed to assess it, with key values, units, and conditions in the prose and source links for traceability. Build later sections on established evidence instead of restating the conclusion in findings, next steps, and lessons. Deliver ready-to-use prose; a summary cites existing Runs and creates none. A drafted Feishu report is text to paste; sending it is a separate, explicitly authorized action.
